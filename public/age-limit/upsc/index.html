UPSC age limit at a glance

You must be at least 21 and under 32 on 1 August of the exam year. Reserved categories get extra years on the upper limit: 3 for OBC, 5 for SC/ST and 10 for persons with benchmark disabilities (13 for OBC and 15 for SC/ST PwBD candidates). Ex-servicemen get 5 years and defence personnel disabled in operations 3 years, with conditions set out in the notice.

How UPSC counts your age

Age is counted on 1 August of the exam year, not on the exam date. “21 to 32” means you must have turned 21 by that day and must not yet have turned 32. The calculator above works out the exact range of birth dates for your category and every exam year you fit, assuming the rules stay the same.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Clear answers about this calculator and how to understand its results.

What is the age limit for UPSC CSE 2027?

If the 2026 rules continue, General and EWS candidates must be at least 21 and under 32 on 1 August 2027, which means born between 2 August 1995 and 1 August 2006. UPSC plans to publish the CSE 2027 notification on 13 January 2027.

Can I apply if I turn 32 on 1 August?

No. The notice says you “must not have attained the age of 32 years on the 1st of August”, so turning 32 on that day makes you ineligible for that year.

What is the UPSC age limit for OBC and SC/ST?

OBC candidates get 3 extra years (under 35) and SC/ST candidates 5 extra years (under 37). For CSE 2026 that means OBC candidates born on or after 2 August 1991 and SC/ST candidates born on or after 2 August 1989.

How many attempts do I get?

Six for General and EWS, nine for OBC, and unlimited for SC/ST, all within the age limit. PwBD candidates in General, EWS or OBC get nine. You need both: an attempt left and an age within the limit.

Which date of birth does UPSC accept?

The one recorded in your Class 10 (matriculation) or equivalent certificate. UPSC does not accept a change of date of birth later.

Exam categories and recruitment rules

Civil Services and Administration

Union Public Service Commission recruitments establish strict age limits anchored to 1 August of the exam year. For IAS, IPS, and IFS (Indian Forest Service), the baseline bracket is 21 to 32 years for unreserved candidates. Candidates from Other Backward Classes receive 3 years of relaxation up to 35, while Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes receive 5 years up to 37. Candidates with benchmark disabilities receive 10 years of age relaxation up to 42. UPSC Combined Medical Services (CMS) recruits medical officers under 32 years of age on 1 August, with identical category concessions.

Staff Selection Commission

The Staff Selection Commission manages recruitment for non-gazetted central government posts across multiple education tiers. SSC CHSL recruits Class 12 qualified candidates between 18 and 27 years. SSC CGL groups graduate posts into distinct age tiers: 18 to 27 years for clerical and junior auditor roles, 20 to 30 years for Section Officers and Central Excise Inspectors, and up to 32 years for Junior Statistical Officers. SSC GD Constable recruits young aspirants between 18 and 23 years into central paramilitary forces including BSF, CISF, CRPF, SSB, and ITBP.

Banking and Financial Sector

Public sector bank recruitments conducted by SBI and IBPS enforce strict age rules tied to graduation. Probationary Officer exams (SBI PO and IBPS PO) require candidates to be between 21 and 30 years (IBPS PO accepts from age 20). Clerical recruitment (SBI Junior Associates and IBPS Clerk) sets a narrower bracket of 20 to 28 years. Across all banking exams, OBC candidates get 3 years relaxation, SC and ST candidates get 5 years, and PwD candidates get 10 years. In addition, SBI permits attempts for widows and divorced women up to age 35.

Defence and Uniformed Services

Indian Armed Forces entries enforce exact born-between windows rather than conventional caste-based age relaxations. The Agnipath scheme sets a uniform bracket of 17.5 to 21 years across the Indian Army, Indian Navy (SSR and MR), and Air Force Agniveervayu. Candidates must be under 21 years on the day of official enrolment. For commissioned officer entries, NDA recruits candidates between 16.5 and 19.5 years. CDS recruits graduates between 19 and 24 years for the Indian Military Academy and Naval Academy, 20 to 24 for the Air Force Academy, and 19 to 25 for the Officers Training Academy. AFCAT admits flying branch applicants from 20 to 24 years, with an extension up to 26 years for DGCA Commercial Pilot Licence holders.

Railways and Technical Cadres

Railway Recruitment Boards manage massive recruitment drives across both graduate and technical cadres. RRB NTPC establishes age limits of 18 to 30 years for undergraduate posts and 18 to 33 years for graduate posts such as Station Master and Goods Train Manager. In recent Centralized Employment Notices, the Ministry of Railways added a 3-year age relaxation across all categories as a relief measure following recruitment delays. Railway Assistant Loco Pilot (ALP) recruits technical diploma and ITI holders between 18 and 30 years (extended to 33 with the post-covid concession). RRB Group D Level 1 positions accept applicants between 18 and 33 years.

State Police Recruitment

State police boards set age limits according to state domicile rules. UP Police Constable recruitment sets an age range of 18 to 22 years for male general candidates and 18 to 25 years for female general candidates, with UP Police Sub Inspector set at 21 to 28 years. Candidates belonging to OBC, SC, and ST categories with UP domicile receive 5 years of relaxation. Bihar Police Constable sets 18 to 25 years for general candidates, extending up to 27 for BC and EBC men, 28 for BC and EBC women, and 30 for SC and ST candidates. Most Indian states adhere to a baseline of 18 to 25 years for constabulary recruitment, accompanied by mandatory physical efficiency testing.

Teaching and Academic Eligibility

Academic testing focuses on qualification benchmarks rather than upper age barriers. UGC NET awards Junior Research Fellowships (JRF) to scholars not older than 30 years on the first day of the exam month, with 5 years relaxation granted to women, OBC-NCL, SC, ST, and PwD researchers. Eligibility for Assistant Professor appointments carries no upper age limit whatsoever. Similarly, the Central Teacher Eligibility Test (CTET) requires a minimum age of 18 years but imposes no upper age ceiling, and the awarded qualifying certificate remains valid for life.

Higher Education and Entrance Examinations

Major national entrance examinations for professional higher education have systematically eliminated upper age restrictions to facilitate lifelong learning. GATE, CAT, CUET UG and PG, CLAT UG and PG, and NEET (both UG and PG) impose no upper age limit. For JEE Main, candidates must have passed Class 12 in the exam year or preceding two years. For NEET UG, students must have completed 17 years of age on or before 31 December of the admission year. While entrance tests admit candidates of any age, participating colleges or hiring PSUs may enforce distinct age criteria during post-exam selection.

How cutoff dates and age limits work

Indian government notifications state the age limit as a requirement to be met on a specific calendar cutoff date. Understanding how this date interacts with your birth certificate is critical before submitting an application.

The rule of “must not have attained”

Notifications frequently specify that a candidate “must have attained the age of 21 years and must not have attained the age of 32 years on the 1st of August”. In Indian administrative law, this means you must be at least 21 years old on that day, and you must not have celebrated your 32nd birthday on or before 1 August. If your 32nd birthday falls on 1 August, you are considered to have attained 32 years on that date and are ineligible for the unreserved category.

The matriculation certificate rule

Recruiting agencies including UPSC, SSC, RRB, and state boards accept only the date of birth recorded in your Class 10 (matriculation or secondary education board) certificate. Affidavits, municipal birth certificates registered years later, or horoscope records cannot override the matriculation record once submitted.
